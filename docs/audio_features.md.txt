# Audio Features and Mood Patterns

## 1. Objective
This analysis investigates the distributions and relationships of specific audio features within the Spotify Tracks dataset. The core research questions are:
* How are energy, danceability, and valence distributed across the tracks?
* Which audio features appear mathematically related to one another?
* Do tracks with higher energy generally exhibit higher danceability?

## 2. Dataset and Variables
The analysis utilizes `spotify-tracks-dataset.csv`. To isolate this specific scope, the dataset was subset to the following variables:
* `track_name` & `artists`: Track identifiers.
* `danceability`: Suitability for dancing based on tempo, rhythm stability, and beat strength (0.0 to 1.0).
* `energy`: Perceptual measure of intensity and activity (0.0 to 1.0).
* `valence`: Musical positiveness conveyed by a track (0.0 to 1.0).
* `acousticness`: Confidence measure of whether the track is acoustic (0.0 to 1.0).
* `loudness`: Overall loudness of a track in decibels (dB).

## 3. Data Preparation
Data cleaning was performed using `pandas` to ensure accurate statistical distributions:
1. **Index Cleanup:** Dropped legacy index artifacts (`Unnamed: 0` and `Unnamed: 0.1`).
2. **Column Isolation:** Filtered the dataframe to only include the relevant audio features required for Member 1.
3. **Targeted Null Removal:** Removed 1 row missing `track_name` and `artists`. 
4. **Duplicate Removal:** Removed 27,268 exact duplicate rows to prevent artificial skewing of feature histograms.

## 4. Exploratory Data Analysis
Visualizations were generated using `matplotlib` and `seaborn`. 
* **Distributions:** Histograms with Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) overlays were plotted for all five numeric features.
* **Energy vs. Danceability:** A custom scatter plot utilizing marker size reduction (`s=2`) and heavy alpha blending (`alpha=0.02`) was engineered to map density dynamically, resolving severe overplotting.
* **Relationships:** A Pearson correlation heatmap was generated for all numeric variables.

## 6. Limitations
* **Causation Fallacy:** Strong correlations (e.g., between energy and loudness) do not establish that one feature directly causes the other; both may be heavily influenced by modern mixing and mastering standards.
* **Dataset Bias:** The dataset includes multiple genres, meaning the distributions represent global averages rather than genre-specific trends (which are covered in Member 3's analysis).
